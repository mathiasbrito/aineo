## The fault

`tests/test_claude.lua` › *quitting Neovim* › *ends a turn by keys though Ctrl-C is pressed in the editor meanwhile* failed on `dev` with the fake ending `{ ended = 'hangup', code = 129 }` and no `{ turn = 'interrupted' }` before it: the editor had quit without pressing a key in Claude Code's terminal.

## What was measured

The case was cut into a file of its own, after the file's first case of that group (*stops an idle Claude by a double Ctrl-C*), which keeps a session id in the test file's home — so that the case's start resumes, as it does in the whole file.

| Run | Fails |

| `dev`, the case after the idle case (a resume), several series at different loads | 2 of 20, 3 of 20, 3 of 25, 2 of 30, 11 of 30, 6 of 30, 10 of 30, 4 of 40 (some series with logging added to the cut file) |

In a failing run the editor's `-V9` log (a wrapper script around `nvim` as the child, which also showed every child exit with code 0 and no crash report) ends after `BufUnload` of the terminal, with no `VimLeavePre`: the editor quit without running the stop (`stop.stop_by_keys()`), and its exit hung Claude Code up. A logger in `QuitPre`, `ExitPre` and `VimLeavePre` of the child wrote nothing in a failing run, and a log in the stop wrote nothing.

## The cause

The test's: `claude.quit_pressing_ctrl_c(child)` sent `:qa` and, in the same breath, the first of its Ctrl-C presses. A Ctrl-C that reaches the editor in the first milliseconds of its `:qa` can make it quit without running its `VimLeavePre` handlers, so the stop never starts. The stop is not at fault: a Ctrl-C pressed while it waits, which is what the case means by "meanwhile", is handled (`stop.wait_for_exit()`), as every passing run shows.

Not isolated: why T48 part 1 raised the rate (5 of 40 against 0 of 40 beside it). T48's waiting start adds a `BufUnload` autocommand to the start's buffer, but taking it out left 4 of 40 failing (the mutant is in the table below), so it is not that one. What `v0.2.28` does differently was not found; the figure is recorded as measured. Also not isolated: the mechanism inside Neovim by which an early Ctrl-C skips `VimLeavePre` — a deterministic reproduction (a slow `QuitPre` or `BufUnload` handler, a Ctrl-C sent while it blocks) did not fail. A user typing Ctrl-C within milliseconds of `:qa` meets the same editor; the hangup then ends Claude Code (code 129) where the keys would have, and nothing outlives the editor.

## The fix

`claude.quit_pressing_ctrl_c(child, fake)` (`tests/helpers/claude_session.lua`) sends `:qa` and presses Ctrl-C in the editor only once the fake has received the stop's first Ctrl-C (`claude.ctrl_c_count(fake) > 0`) — a condition on what the stop has done, bounded by the same `STOP_PATIENCE_MS` wait. Both callers in `tests/test_claude.lua` pass their fake. What the cases assert is unchanged, and the presses still run through the stop's waits.

After the fix, on `dev`: the cut file 40 of 40 loaded and 10 of 10 alone; the whole `tests/test_claude.lua` 2 of 2 loaded and 4 of 4 alone (see *Whole file*).
