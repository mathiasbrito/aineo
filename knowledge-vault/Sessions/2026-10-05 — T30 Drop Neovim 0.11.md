## What was done

**A — the eight test branches (kind 1: the 0.12 side kept).**
- `tests/test_entry.lua`: `TERMOPEN_FAILURE`, `TERMOPEN_FAILURE_QUOTING_FRAMING`, `BUFFILEPRE_FAILURE`, `TERMOPEN_FILE_FAILURE_NAMING_A_POSITION` and `TERMOPEN_FILE_FAILURE_ON_A_LATER_LINE` are now plain strings. Their docstrings speak of Neovim's words, not each Neovim's.
- `tests/test_claude.lua`: `REQUEST_ERROR_FRAMING` is `'^Lua: '`. `wait_for_exit_line()` lost its early return, and its docstring and `EXIT_LINE_TEXTS`'s no longer name a version.
- `tests/test_mcp_delivery.lua`: `BUFFILEPRE_REASON` is a plain string.

**B — the 0.11 error framing (kind 2).** Probe B (`t30-probes.txt`) raised an error in Lua through nine paths on 0.12.5, five in the process and four over RPC to a second 0.12.5. RPC `nvim_exec_lua` is what the relay asks its editor. **No first line held `Error executing lua`** (0 of 9). The 0.12.5 binary holds no `Error executing` (`grep -a -c` → 0), though it does hold `Lua callback: ` (→ 1, the positive control). The pattern is gone from `plugin/aineo.lua`'s and `lua/aineo/mcp/editor.lua`'s `ERROR_FRAMING`, and their docstrings no longer name Neovim versions. The parametrized framing case in `tests/test_mcp_delivery.lua` is now one case, *a report › that the editor refuses is a tool error with its reason, without Neovim’s framing*, framed with `Lua: `. A parametrize set with one row is a case.

**Finding, against the brief review's measurement:** the reason `run_within_bound()` gives is not 0.11-only. On this host's 0.12.5, while `yes` (and `yes flood`) wrote to a stdout callback, `vim.wait(1000, …)` returned only after 10.9–11.0 s, once a watchdog timer had killed the writer. That held with and without `fast_only`, with a counting handler and with a no-op one. `SystemObj:wait(1000)` returned after 10.0 s. That is two runs with three rows each, plus the two no-op rows. The brief review measured 1002 ms and 1003 ms. Its probe file is not in the evidence, so the difference was not traced. The docstring names no version, and it is true on 0.12.5 as measured here, so it was left as it is.

**What stays, as the brief says:** the provenance lines `readiness.lua:4` and `stop.lua:13`, and the fixture's header.

**D — the timing helper's LuaJIT check (kind 2).** Probe D started 300 fresh children as the helper starts them (`children.restart()`) and ran the helper's `COMPILES_CODE` verbatim in each. **0 of 300 compiled nothing.** In the positive control, a child given `jit.off()` and `jit.flush()` held no trace. Removed from `tests/helpers/timed_attempts.lua`:
- `STARTS_FOR_A_COMPILING_CHILD`, `COMPILES_CODE` and `start_compiling_child()`, and their docstring lines. `within_limit()` now runs `in_a_home_of_its_own(timing.start)`.

Removed from `tests/test_timed_attempts.lua`:
- the two cases under *a child*, and the set;
- their two helpers, `start_compiling_on_even_starts_after()` and `child_holds_a_trace()`.

This makes a clause of MR212 false: "where five starts give one, a LuaJIT that compiles code" in RP4's reading, which the user accepted on 2026-10-05. The knowledge pass corrects MR212, and the orchestrator tells the user.

`within_limit()` no longer uses its `child` parameter. Its callers in `tests/test_report_paths.lua` and `tests/test_report_links.lua` lie outside the boundary, so the parameter is kept as `_child` (selene's unused-variable rule) and its docstring says the attempts reach the child only through `timing`. Dropping it is a follow-up that touches those two files.

No code path starts one so today.
